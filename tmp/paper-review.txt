ABSTRACT


Title of research:A Centralized Digital Multi-Branch Platform for the Leo Mejillano Salon and Beauty Services Brand

Researcher:                         Michael Kharl M. Agao
                                            Paulo M. Balaguer
                                            Angelo O. Navarra
Degree:                               Bachelor of Science in Information Technology

Key words:Salon Management, Multi-Branch System, Appointment  Booking, Inventory Monitoring

This study presents a centralized web-based platform developed for Leo Mejillano Salon and Beauty Services to unify operations across its branches in Daraga, Legazpi City, and Cabangan, Albay. The goal is to replace manual, branch-by-branch processes with a single system that streamlines appointment booking, payment handling, inventory monitoring, and staff management. Clients can book services online as registered customers or as guests using only a name and contact number, pay reservation deposits through an integrated online payment gateway, and reschedule, track, and review their appointments. Front desk cashiers verify payments, manage the appointment pipeline, process checkouts, and issue receipts, while staff monitor their assignments, attendance, and supply usage. For the owner, the platform provides a unified dashboard, branch-specific service and promotion management, booking limit configuration, staff performance rankings, and consolidated reports. The development process includes user feedback and testing to ensure a user-friendly experience across desktop and mobile devices. In essence, the platform aims to improve operational efficiency and customer experience, and to support data-driven decisions for the owner across all branch locations.

Introduction
Project Context
The beauty and wellness industry plays an important role in providing employment and serving the daily needs of consumers around the world. As businesses in this sector expand to multiple locations, the proper management of appointments, staff schedules, inventory, and operational records becomes increasingly important to maintaining  consistent service quality and long-term client satisfaction. Across different markets, enterprises are recognizing that relying on manual processes alone is no longer sufficient to meet the demands of a competitive environment. Scholars in business and information systems emphasize that the adoption of digital platforms enables organizations to streamline their operations, reduce human error, and make well-informed decisions based on current and accurate data. This global shift toward digitalization in service-oriented industries highlights the growing need for integrated systems that can support not only the present requirements of a business but also its capacity for future growth.  
In the Philippine context, small and medium enterprises in the beauty and wellness sector are increasingly facing pressure to modernize their operations as client expectations rise and market competition intensifies. Many local salon and personal care businesses have begun adopting digital tools to manage bookings, track supplies, and coordinate with their teams. However, a significant number of enterprises, particularly those managing more than one branch, still depend on manual and paper-based methods that are prone to inconsistency and error. The Department of Trade and Industry (DTI) has consistently encouraged Philippine small businesses to embrace digital transformation as a means of improving competitiveness and sustaining growth in an evolving market. The Leo Mejillano brand is one such enterprise. With three established locations in Albay the Leo Mejillano Salon and Make Up Studio in Daraga, Skin Brows by Leo Mejillano in Yashano, and Lash and Brows in Cabangan the brand has grown considerably in reach and clientele, yet it continues to operate without a unified system for managing its daily activities across all branches.Currently, scheduling at each Leo Mejillano branch is handled manually and independently, which frequently results in booking conflicts, missed appointments, and inconsistencies in client records. These shortages can cause cancelled appointments, lost revenue, and a diminished experience for clients. Monitoring staff performance and reviewing branch-level sales also requires management to visit each location in person, which is time-consuming and increasingly impractical as the brand continues to grow. According to Laudon and Laudon (2020), integrated information systems help businesses consolidate data, eliminate redundancy, and reduce the errors that arise from fragmented, manual record-keeping. The operational difficulties currently faced by the Leo Mejillano brand are a direct consequence of the absence of such a system. 
Beyond its internal operations, the brand also faces a widening gap between what modern clients expect and what the current setup can deliver. Clients today expect the convenience of viewing available services and booking appointments quickly and easily, without having to make phone calls or visit a branch in person. This level of accessibility has become a standard expectation in the beauty and wellness industry, and businesses that cannot offer it risk losing clients to competitors who have already adopted digital booking platforms. In a 2023 report by Statista, a growing number of consumers across Southeast Asia indicated that they prefer to book personal care and grooming services through online platforms rather than through traditional walk-in or phone-based methods, reflecting a clear shift in client behavior that local businesses must address. When a business fails to meet this expectation, the consequences extend beyond lost bookings they include damaged reputation, reduced client loyalty, and a weakened position in an increasingly digital marketplace. Furthermore, the inability to provide real-time service information and online scheduling forces potential clients to seek out competitors who can offer these conveniences with ease. For a growing brand like Leo Mejillano, this gap represents not only a present operational weakness but also a significant barrier to attracting and retaining a wider client base. Addressing this gap is therefore not simply a matter of modernizing the brand's image but a practical and necessary step toward ensuring its long-term viability and competitiveness in the local beauty and wellness market. In response to these challenges, the development of a centralized, web-based management system tailored to the specific needs of the Leo Mejillano brand presents a viable and timely solution. Such a system would unify appointment scheduling, staff coordination, inventory tracking. 
Purpose and Description
The project develops a centralized web-based platform that serves as the main system for organizing and monitoring the daily operations of Leo Mejillano Salon & Makeup Studio (Daraga Branch), Skin Brows by Leo Mejillano (Yashano Branch), Lash & Brows bye Leo Mejillano Salon (Cabangan Branch).Its primary function is to bring together appointment scheduling, inventory tracking, staff performance recording. Instead of managing each branch separately through manual methods, the platform allows all locations to be viewed and managed through a single dashboard, giving the management team a complete and current picture of operations at any time without the need for physical branch visits. A key feature of the system is its guest booking function, which allows clients to reserve appointments at any branch by simply entering their name and contact number, removing the inconvenience of phone-based scheduling and making the process faster and more accessible for everyone.
What makes this platform relevant and practical is that it is designed specifically around the structure and needs of the Leo Mejillano brand, a multi-branch beauty and wellness enterprise operating within the local setting of Albay. Unlike general-purpose booking or scheduling applications, this system is built to reflect how the brand actually operates, accommodating the distinct services offered at each branch while maintaining one unified record system for the entire enterprise. It is straightforward enough to be used by both clients and staff without requiring any technical knowledge, making it immediately useful to its intended users. By replacing the brand's current fragmented and manual processes with a single organized digital solution, the platform provides a dependable and accessible foundation for improving day-to-day consistency, reducing scheduling and inventory errors, and supporting the continued growth of the Leo Mejillano brand across all of Leo Mejillano Salon & Makeup Studio (Daraga Branch), Skin Brows by Leo Mejillano (Yashano Branch), Lash & Brows bye Leo Mejillano Salon (Cabangan Branch). This digital transition empowers the business to scale its operations while ensuring that high-quality service remains synonymous with the brand across every location. The platform also offers real-time updates on inventory levels, allowing for timely stock replenishment and reducing the risk of shortages, which ensures uninterrupted service delivery. 
Objectives
General Objectives:
To develop a centralized web-based multi-branch Salon Management System for Leo Mejillano Salon that improves scheduling, inventory, staff management, and customer service through automation and data updates.
Specific Objectives:
To develop a centralized web-based platform that allows customers to select their preferred branch location in Albay (Yashano Branch, Cabangan Branch, and Daraga Branch) and view available services, schedules, and branch-specific offerings. 
To implement a guest booking system that allows customers to book appointments using only their name and contact number.
To develop an appointment management and tracking system that allows customers to monitor booking status, receive service updates, and access appointment progress.
To develop a secure administrative system with role-based access for the owner, front desk/cashier, and staff to manage operations, staff assignments, and branch records. 
To implement a centralized inventory tracking system for monitoring beauty products and professional supplies across all branches.
To provide an administrative dashboard for the owner to monitor schedules, bookings, branch performance, and overall business operations through a single login.
To integrate a home service booking system that allows administrators to schedule, organize, and assign staff for off-site appointments.
To implement an automated notification system that sends appointment reminders and service updates to customers via SMS or email.
To create a feedback and rating module that allows customers to evaluate services and branch performance.
To implement employee accounts per branch for managing attendance, task assignments, and workforce allocation, and to develop a performance evaluation system based on completed services, customer satisfaction, and attendance.
To enable the owner to manage and customize service menus and promotions per branch, with options to add, update, or remove based on business needs and local demand.
Scope and Limitations
Scope
This study covers the design, development, and implementation of a centralized web-based platform for Leo Mejillano Salon and Beauty Services. The study aims to streamline appointment booking, inventory monitoring, staff management, payment recording, and branch performance tracking across its locations in Daraga, Legazpi City, and Cabangan, Albay. The intended users and beneficiaries include the owner, front desk personnel or cashiers, staff members assigned to each branch, and clients who book appointments through the platform.
The proposed platform will include the following features: multi-branch service selection and appointment booking with a guest booking option requiring only a name and contact number; a home service booking module for off-site appointments; a role-based administrative system with distinct access levels for the owner (administrator), front desk/cashier, and staff; online reservation payments through PayMongo, along with cashier payment verification, checkout, and receipt generation; appointment rescheduling and a reservation deposit policy; a multi-branch inventory tracking module maintained through staff input; automated booking updates and appointment reminders through email and in-app notifications; a feedback and rating module for customer evaluations; a staff performance ranking system using one selected metric at a time—services completed, average customer rating, or attendance rate; individual employee accounts per branch for attendance and task monitoring; branch-specific service menu and promotional offer management; a customer loyalty program; configurable booking limits to manage appointment capacity and prevent scheduling conflicts; and a unified owner dashboard with reports for monitoring schedules, bookings, and overall branch performance across all locations.
The platform will be accessible through compatible web browsers on desktop and mobile devices with a stable internet connection, allowing staff and clients to interact with the system conveniently. Through a single centralized interface, the owner will have access to consolidated operational records and performance summaries from all branches. These features will support informed decision-making by helping the owner identify operational inefficiencies, monitor key performance indicators, and make adjustments to improve business operations.

Limitations
The system is developed exclusively for Leo Mejillano Salon and Beauty Services and is not intended for use by other salon or wellness businesses. Adapting the platform for other enterprises would require a separate study on generalized system architecture, which falls outside the objectives of this capstone project.

The automated notification feature relies on a third-party email service through SMTP to deliver booking, payment, and reminder messages. SMS notifications are not implemented in the current version because they require a paid SMS provider account. The system only prepares and logs SMS reminder content for future integration. Email delivery depends on external service availability and reliability, which are beyond the researchers’ control; therefore, successful delivery cannot be guaranteed.

Staff performance rankings are based solely on records entered into the platform. The system ranks staff using one selected metric at a time: services completed, average customer rating, or attendance rate. It does not generate a combined weighted performance score or account for qualitative performance factors that are not captured within the platform.

The platform does not include payroll processing or financial accounting features. Although it supports checkout and payment recording, these functions do not constitute a complete accounting system. Payroll and financial accounting require specialized functionality beyond the scope of this capstone project.

The accuracy and reliability of inventory tracking depend on the consistent and proper recording of stock movements by designated staff at each branch. The system cannot automatically detect physical stock changes that are not entered into the platform. Unrecorded or incorrectly entered transactions may therefore result in discrepancies between recorded inventory and actual stock levels.

The platform includes checkout and payment recording and integrates with a single online payment gateway, PayMongo, for reservation payments through GCash, Maya, and cards. However, it does not integrate with external point-of-sale (POS) hardware or software or with accounting systems. Transactions processed through a separate POS must be manually reconciled with the platform’s records. Available online payment methods are limited to those supported by PayMongo and enabled for the integration, while processing, settlement, and refund timelines depend on the payment provider.

Methodology
This study employed the Rapid Application Development (RAD) model as its software development methodology. RAD is an iterative development approach that emphasizes rapid prototyping, incremental delivery, and direct user involvement throughout the development process. The model was first formalized by James Martin in the 1990s as a structured alternative to traditional sequential development methods, which were often ill-suited to projects requiring frequent requirement changes and active stakeholder participation.
The RAD model was adopted in this study on the basis that the Leo Mejillano Salon and Beauty Services Brand operates across three geographically separate branches — located in Daraga, Yashano, and Cabangan in Albay — each of which currently manages appointments, inventory, and staff records through manual and uncoordinated methods. The absence of a unified digital system has resulted in scheduling conflicts, inconsistent client records, and operational inefficiencies that cannot be adequately addressed through a rigid, documentation-heavy development process.

Figure 1.0 Rapid Application Development (RAD) Cycle
Figure 1.0 illustrates the Rapid Application Development cycle adopted in this study. The cycle begins with Analysis and Quick Design, where the needs and constraints of the system are identified and a preliminary design is developed. This is followed by the Prototype Cycles phase, which operates as a continuous loop of three iterative sub-phases: Demonstrate, where the working prototype is presented to the stakeholders for evaluation; Refine, where the system is revised based on the feedback received; and Build, where the next version of the prototype is constructed. The loop continues until the system satisfies the operational requirements of the Leo Mejillano Salon and Beauty Services Brand. The cycle then proceeds to the Testing phase, where all system modules are evaluated for functionality, accuracy, and usability. The cycle concludes with the Implementation phase, where the completed system is deployed, users are trained, and the platform is formally transitioned into active use.
Analysis and Quick Design
The first phase of the RAD methodology involved a systematic analysis of the current operational conditions of the Leo Mejillano Salon and Beauty Services Brand, followed by the development of a preliminary system design. This phase established the informational and structural foundation upon which the prototype cycles were subsequently conducted.
Requirements Gathering

The proponents conducted requirements gathering through three data collection methods: interviews, direct observation, and document analysis. These methods were applied across all three Leo Mejillano branch locations to ensure that the system requirements identified were representative of the full operational scope of the brand.
Interviews
Structured interviews were conducted with the salon owner, Mr. Leopoldo Mejillano, and with front desk personnel and salon staff from the Daraga, Yashano, and Cabangan branches. The interviews were directed at obtaining detailed information regarding the current processes for appointment booking, inventory monitoring, staff assignment, and inter-branch coordination. Findings indicated that appointments are managed through phone calls and walk-in arrangements, that inventory is monitored manually without a shared tracking system, and that the evaluation of staff performance across branches necessitates physical visits by the owner to each location.
Direct Observation
On-site observation was conducted at all three branches to verify the information obtained through interviews and to document actual workflows under operating conditions. The proponents observed the handling of appointment scheduling during high-volume periods, the management of concurrent bookings by front desk personnel, and the communication of inventory-related concerns between branch locations. The observations confirmed that the current manual processes result in scheduling conflicts, duplicated entries in appointment records, and delays in the dissemination of operational information across branches.
Document Analysis 
Existing logbooks, appointment records, and transaction summaries from the branches were reviewed to identify patterns of error and inconsistency in the current record-keeping practices. The analysis provided documentary evidence of the operational limitations arising from the absence of a centralized information system and served to inform the specification of functional requirements for the proposed platform.
Identification of System Requirement

Based on the data obtained through interviews, observation, and document analysis, the proponents identified the functional requirements of the Centralized Digital Multi-Branch Platform. The system is required to provide multi-branch appointment booking with a guest booking option that accepts only a client name and contact number, a home service booking module for the management of off-site appointments, and a role-based administrative system with distinct access levels for the owner, front desk personnel, and salon staff. The system must further include a multi-branch inventory tracking module maintained through staff input, an automated notification function for the delivery of appointment reminders via SMS or email, and a feedback and rating module through which clients may evaluate services rendered. Additional requirements include a staff performance ranking system based on services completed, attendance, and customer satisfaction scores; individual employee accounts per branch for the recording of attendance and task assignments; branch-specific service menu and promotional offer management; and a unified owner dashboard through which the performance and operations of all three branches may be monitored from a single interface.
Quick Design
Following the identification of requirements, the proponents developed a preliminary design of the system architecture and user interface. The quick design was not intended to be a final or exhaustive specification but rather a working framework to guide the initial prototype build. It defined the general structure of the platform, including the separation between the client-facing appointment booking interface and the role-based administrative back-end, the data relationships among the three branch modules, and the navigation pathways assigned to each user role. This design served as the baseline reference for the first cycle of prototype development.
Prototype Cycles
The Prototype Cycles phase constitutes the central and most iterative component of the RAD methodology applied in this study. Rather than developing the complete system in a single continuous process, the proponents constructed the platform incrementally through a series of repeating cycles. Each cycle consisted of three sequential sub-phases Demonstrate, Refine, and Build  which were repeated until a stable and fully functional version of the system was achieved.
Demonstrate
Each prototype cycle commenced with a demonstration of the current working version of the system to the project stakeholders, including the salon owner, Mr. Leopoldo Mejillano, and designated front desk and salon staff personnel from the Leo Mejillano branches. The demonstration sessions provided stakeholders with direct access to the prototype, allowing them to interact with its features and communicate their observations regarding the accuracy, completeness, and usability of the system in relation to actual branch operations. The demonstrations served the dual function of verifying that the implemented features corresponded to the requirements established during the analysis phase and of providing a structured mechanism through which end users could identify deficiencies or inconsistencies that required correction prior to the subsequent build.
Refine
Subsequent to each demonstration session, the proponents conducted a systematic review of the feedback received and documented all identified issues according to their nature and priority. Necessary modifications were then incorporated into the system before the Build sub-phase of the same cycle commenced. Refinements across the prototype cycles included revisions to the appointment booking interface to improve accessibility for users without prior experience with digital platforms, adjustments to the inventory tracking module to accurately reflect the product categories in use at each branch, modifications to the administrative dashboard to enhance the legibility of branch performance data, and the introduction of booking limit controls to prevent the recurrence of scheduling conflicts observed during the analysis phase.
Build
The Build sub-phase involved the construction of the revised prototype incorporating all refinements identified in the preceding demonstration and refine stages. The system was developed using Visual Studio Code as the integrated development environment, MySQL as the relational database management system, and HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and Tailwind CSS as the frontend development technologies. Development proceeded in a modular sequence, with each build cycle directed at a defined cluster of system functionalities.
The initial build cycle established the foundational structure of the platform, encompassing the multi-branch selection interface and the guest booking module, which permits clients to reserve appointments by submitting only their name and contact number. This module was accorded development priority given that it directly addresses the most critical operational deficiency identified during the analysis phase: the dependence on unstructured, phone-based booking arrangements that generate frequent scheduling conflicts. Subsequent build cycles incorporated the remaining system modules, including the role-based access control system, the inventory tracking module, the staff performance ranking system, the automated notification feature, the feedback and rating module, and the unified owner dashboard. Each module was developed within a multi-branch architectural framework to ensure that data from the Daraga, Yashano, and Cabangan branches could be accessed and managed through a single centralized interface. Upon completion of each Build sub-phase, the cycle returned to the Demonstrate stage, and the iterative process continued until all modules were functional, tested within the cycle, and approved by the stakeholders.
Testing
Following the completion of the prototype cycles and the attainment of a stable system version, the proponents conducted a formal testing phase to assess the functionality, reliability, and usability of the platform prior to deployment.
Functional Testing
Functional testing was conducted to verify that each system module operated in accordance with the specified requirements. The guest booking module was tested to confirm that appointments could be reserved using only a client name and contact number without generating conflicts or errors in the scheduling system. The role-based access control system was evaluated to ensure that each designated user type owner, front desk personnel, and salon staff was restricted to the functions and data relevant to the assigned role. The inventory tracking module was tested to confirm that stock entries submitted by staff at any branch were reflected accurately and in real time within the unified system. The automated notification function was assessed to verify that appointment reminders were transmitted to clients via SMS or email at the appropriate trigger points. The feedback and rating module was examined to confirm that client evaluations were accurately recorded, stored, and rendered within the administrative interface. The staff performance ranking system was tested to verify that rankings were computed correctly based on the defined criteria of services completed, customer satisfaction scores, and attendance records. The owner dashboard was evaluated to confirm that real-time operational data from all three branch locations was accurately consolidated and presented through a single administrative interface.
User Acceptance Testing
User Acceptance Testing was conducted with the actual end users of the system, comprising the salon owner, front desk personnel, and salon staff from the Daraga, Yashano, and Cabangan branches. Participants were assigned a series of representative operational tasks to perform using the platform, including reserving an appointment as a client, submitting an inventory update as a staff member, and reviewing consolidated branch performance data as the owner. The responses and interactions of participants were documented and analyzed to assess the usability, operational adequacy, and overall suitability of the system for its intended function. Issues identified during this phase were addressed through targeted modifications to ensure that the system was in its most complete and operationally appropriate state prior to final deployment.
Implementation
The Implementation phase involved the formal deployment of the Centralized Digital Multi-Branch Platform across all three Leo Mejillano branch locations: Leo Mejillano Salon and Makeup Studio in Daraga, Skin Brows by Leo Mejillano in Yashano, and Lash and Brows in Cabangan. This phase encompassed the transition from the existing manual operational processes to the centralized digital system and constituted the final stage of the RAD development cycle.
Prior to deployment, the proponents conducted orientation and training sessions for all system users, including the salon owner, front desk personnel, and salon staff. The sessions were structured to ensure that each user group was able to perform the functions specific to their assigned role within the system and to address operational questions arising from the transition to a digital platform. Instructional materials were developed in consideration of the varying levels of digital literacy among personnel, as a portion of the salon staff had limited prior experience with web-based management tools.
Technical Background
Technologies to be Used
Development Tools and Environment

Visual Studio Code, commonly known as VS Code, is one of the most popular and widely used Integrated Development Environments in the world today. It was created by Microsoft and released to the public for free, making it accessible to beginner programmers and professional developers alike. An Integrated Development Environment, or IDE, is simply a software application that provides a complete workspace for writing, editing, testing, and debugging code all in one place. Think of it as a very smart and powerful text editor that understands programming languages and helps you write better code faster.
Database Management System
MySQL is one of the oldest, most reliable, and most widely used database management systems in the entire world. It is an open-source relational database, which means it is free to use and organizes data into structured tables made up of rows and columns, similar to how a spreadsheet works. MySQL was originally developed in the mid-1990s and has since become the backbone of countless websites, applications, and businesses around the world, including large companies like Facebook, Twitter, and YouTube in their early days.
Frontend Development Technologies 

HTML, which stands for Hypertext Markup Language, is the fundamental building block of every website and web page that exists on the internet today. It is not exactly a programming language in the traditional sense, but rather a markup language, which means it uses special tags and elements to define the structure and content of a web page. Every time you open a website in your browser, what you are seeing is the result of HTML working behind the scenes to organize and display text, images, links, buttons, forms, and all other visual elements on the screen.CSS, which stands for Cascading Style Sheets, is the language responsible for making websites look beautiful, organized, and visually appealing. While HTML provides the raw structure and content of a web page, CSS is what gives that structure its visual appearance, including colors, fonts, sizes, spacing, layout, and overall design. Without CSS, every website would look like a plain, unstilled document with black text on a white background, similar to reading a simple text file with no formatting or decoration whatsoever.
JavaScript is the most widely used programming language in the world and is the only programming language that runs natively inside web browsers, making it absolutely essential for web development. While HTML structures the content of a web page and CSS makes it look attractive, JavaScript is what brings a website to life by adding interactivity, dynamic behavior, and real-time functionality. Without JavaScript, websites would be completely static, meaning they would only display fixed content with no ability to respond to user actions or update information without reloading the entire page.
Framework
Tailwind CSS is a modern and highly popular utility-first CSS framework that has completely changed the way many developers approach styling and designing web interfaces. Unlike traditional CSS frameworks that provide pre-designed components like buttons, cards, and navigation bars, Tailwind CSS takes a different approach by giving developers a large collection of small, single-purpose utility classes that can be combined directly in the HTML to build any design imaginable. This approach gives developers much greater flexibility and control over the appearance of their projects without being locked into a specific visual style.
Authentication
SMS authentication is a security method used by web applications and digital systems to verify the identity of a user by sending a unique code or one-time password directly to their mobile phone number via a text message. This process is commonly known as two-factor authentication, or 2FA, because it adds a second layer of security on top of the traditional username and password login. Even if someone manages to steal or guess your password, they would still be unable to access your account without also having physical access to your phone to receive the verification code.

It also provides helpful features such as syntax highlighting, IntelliSense (code suggestions), debugging tools, and integrated terminal access, which improve developer productivity and reduce errors during the development process.
For data management, the system utilizes MySQL as its database management system. MySQL is a powerful and widely used relational database system designed to store, organize, and manage large amounts of structured data efficiently. It allows the system to perform essential operations such as creating, reading, updating, and deleting records (CRUD operations). MySQL also supports data integrity, security, and relationship management between different tables, ensuring that all stored information remains consistent and reliable. This makes it suitable for applications that require secure and well-organized data handling.
In terms of programming, the system is built using HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, which form the core technologies of modern web development. HTML (HyperText Markup Language) is responsible for structuring the content of the web pages, including headings, forms, tables, and other interface elements. CSS (Cascading Style Sheets) is used to control the visual appearance of the system, such as layout, colors, spacing, typography, and responsiveness across different devices. JavaScript enhances the functionality of the system by adding interactivity and dynamic behavior, such as form validation, real-time updates, and user-triggered events. Together, these three languages create a complete and interactive web-based application.
To improve the design and responsiveness of the user interface, the system incorporates Tailwind CSS as its frontend framework. Tailwind CSS is a utility-first CSS framework that allows developers to style components directly within HTML using predefined classes. This approach speeds up development and ensures design consistency throughout the system. It also makes it easier to create responsive layouts that automatically adapt to different screen sizes, including mobile phones, tablets, and desktops. As a result, the system achieves a modern and visually appealing interface without the need for extensive custom CSS coding.
The user interface itself is primarily built using HTML, which acts as the structural foundation of all web pages. Through HTML elements such as forms, buttons, input fields, menus, and tables, users are able to interact with the system effectively. The interface is designed to be simple, intuitive, and accessible, ensuring that users can easily navigate the system and complete tasks without confusion or difficulty.
For authentication and security, the system employs SMS-based verification. This method enhances account protection by requiring users to verify their identity through a One-Time Password (OTP) sent to their registered mobile number. During login or registration, the system generates a unique code and sends it via SMS, which the user must enter correctly to gain access. This additional security layer helps prevent unauthorized access, reduces the risk of fake accounts, and ensures that only verified users can use the system. In addition to SMS-based verification, the system incorporates multi-factor authentication (MFA) as an added layer of security. This approach requires users to provide additional verification, such as a security question or a secondary email confirmation, alongside the SMS-based OTP. This further strengthens user security by making it significantly harder for unauthorized individuals to gain access, even if they manage to obtain a user’s password. By combining multiple security methods, the platform ensures a higher level of protection for its users and their sensitive data.

Requirements Analysis
The proponents conducted a comprehensive analysis of the current operational processes of the Leo Mejillano Salon and Beauty Services Brand to determine the functional and technical requirements of the proposed Centralized Digital Multi-Branch Platform. The analysis focused on identifying inefficiencies in appointment handling, inventory monitoring, staff coordination, and multi-branch management across the Daraga, Yashano, and Cabangan branches in Albay.
Who
The primary users of the system include the owner, branch managers, front desk/cashier personnel, and salon staff, all of whom are granted role-based administrative access to manage daily operations. The system also serves clients/customers, who are provided with limited access to book appointments, view service availability, and track booking status through a guest or registered user interface.
What
The proposed system is a web-based Salon and Multi-Branch Management System designed to centralize and automate key business operations. It includes modules for online appointment booking, branch selection, inventory management, staff performance tracking, service management, automated notifications, customer feedback and rating, home service booking, and a centralized administrative dashboard. The system also provides real-time monitoring of branch activities and business performance.
Where
The system will be used across all Leo Mejillano Salon branches in Albay, specifically at the Daraga Main Branch, Skin Brows (Yashano Mall), and Lash & Brows (Cabangan). It will be accessible in both on-site salon operations and remote management, allowing administrators to monitor and manage operations even outside physical branch locations using internet-connected devices.

When
The system will be utilized during daily business operations, particularly during peak hours when appointments are actively scheduled and managed. It is also heavily used during high-demand periods such as weekends, holidays, and seasonal promotions, where booking volume increases. Administrators will also use the system regularly for monitoring reports, inventory updates, and performance evaluation.
How
The current workflow of the salon relies heavily on manual appointment recording, phone-based bookings, handwritten logs, and branch-by-branch communication, which often leads to scheduling conflicts, duplicated entries, and delayed information sharing. Inventory tracking is done manually, making it difficult to monitor stock levels across branches in real time.
The proposed system addresses these issues by implementing a centralized digital platform that allows real-time synchronization of appointments, automated scheduling validation to prevent booking conflicts, and a unified inventory tracking system updated by staff input. It also generates automated notifications for clients regarding bookings and reminders, and provides a centralized dashboard for the owner to monitor all branches efficiently. Role-based access control ensures that each user only interacts with relevant system functions based on their position.
Experiment and Tools
To ensure accurate system development, the proponents utilized interviews, direct observation, and document analysis as primary data gathering techniques. Interviews were conducted with the salon owner, front desk/cashier staff, and salon personnel to understand operational challenges in scheduling, inventory handling, and customer management. Direct observation was used to analyze actual day-to-day salon workflows across different branches, particularly in appointment booking and walk-in handling processes. Document analysis was performed on existing logbooks, appointment records, and transaction summaries to identify inconsistencies and inefficiencies in manual record-keeping.
Interview
An interview is a data gathering method where questions are asked directly to people who are familiar with the existing system. It is used to obtain detailed information about processes, problems, and requirements that cannot be fully captured through observation or documents alone.
For this study, structured interviews were conducted with the owner, Mr. Leopoldo Mejillano, as well as front desk/cashier staff and salon personnel from the Leo Mejillano Salon branches in Daraga, Yashano, and Cabangan. The interviews were directed at obtaining detailed information regarding the current processes for appointment booking, inventory monitoring, staff assignment, and inter-branch coordination. The goal was to understand how appointments, customer records, and inventory are currently handled across all three branch locations.
Based on the responses, the current system is mostly manual, relying on phone calls, walk-in bookings, logbooks, and verbal coordination between staff. Appointments are managed through phone calls and walk-in arrangements, while inventory is monitored manually without a shared tracking system. The evaluation of staff performance across branches requires the owner to make physical visits to each location, which is time-consuming and increasingly impractical as the brand continues to grow. This results in frequent booking conflicts, duplicated records, missed appointments, and inconsistent client information across branches. Staff also reported difficulty in checking availability and inventory in real time, especially when multiple branches are involved.
The interviews confirmed the need for a centralized system for scheduling, client records, inventory tracking, and branch management to reduce errors and improve coordination. The findings directly informed the identification of functional requirements for the proposed Centralized Digital Multi-Branch Platform, particularly the need for a guest booking module, role-based access control, and a unified owner dashboard for monitoring all branches from a single interface. These requirements ensure that all operational processes are streamlined and consistently managed across every branch. Ultimately, this centralized approach enhances efficiency, improves decision-making, and supports.
Observation
Observation is a data gathering method where the actual workflow is directly examined without interference. It is used to identify how processes are performed in real situations and to verify issues found during interviews.
On-site observation was conducted in the Daraga, Yashano Mall, and Cabangan branches of Leo Mejillano Salon. The proponents observed the handling of appointment scheduling during high-volume periods, the management of concurrent bookings by front desk personnel, and the communication of inventory-related concerns between branch locations. The proponents observed how appointments, walk-ins, and inventory tasks are handled under actual operating conditions.
It was observed that appointment scheduling is done using manual logbooks and phone calls, which leads to overlapping schedules and delayed updates. The observations confirmed that the current manual processes result in scheduling conflicts, duplicated entries in appointment records, and delays in the dissemination of operational information across branches. Staff must also manually check availability across branches, which slows down the booking process and increases the risk of double-booking, particularly during peak hours and high-demand periods such as weekends and holidays. As a result, customers may experience frustration from either being unable to book appointments or being informed about last-minute cancellations due to overbooking.
Inventory management is also handled manually through physical checking of stock levels, leading to inaccurate records and delayed restocking updates between branches. This becomes more challenging during busy periods, where staff struggle to update records in real time, sometimes resulting in shortages, cancelled appointments.
The observation confirmed that these manual systems are not only time-consuming but also prone to errors, which ultimately affects both operational efficiency and customer satisfaction. Addressing these issues by implementing a more automated, digital system could help streamline processes, reduce errors, and improve communication and data visibility. This would lead to enhanced customer experiences, better inventory management, and more efficient scheduling.
